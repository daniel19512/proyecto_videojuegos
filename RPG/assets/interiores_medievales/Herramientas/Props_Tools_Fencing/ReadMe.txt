WHAT'S IN THIS ZIP
  glTF/      one .glb per model, texture inside. Best for Godot and most engines.
  FBX/       one .fbx per model, for Unity and Unreal.
  Textures/  mdvl_palette.png, the one texture every model uses.
  Contents.csv  every item: name, group, size, triangles.
  License.txt

HOW THE MODELS ARE MADE
- 1 unit = 1 metre. A person is 1.8 m tall.
- Every model uses one material and one 256 x 256 palette texture, the same one
  across the whole library, so they all share a single material in your game.
- Flat shaded: each face is one flat colour. Keep it that way on import.
- Each model's origin is where you place it: on the ground for most, at a grid
  corner for pieces that snap together, at the hand grip for tools and weapons,
  and where it fixes to a wall for things that hang.

IMPORTING
Godot 4: drag the glTF folder in. The texture comes with each model.
Unity: drag the FBX folder and Textures in. Make one material with
  mdvl_palette.png as its base map and give it to the models. On the texture,
  set Filter Mode to Point and Compression to None. On the models, set Normals
  to Calculate and Smoothing Angle to 0.
Unreal 5: import the FBX files and the texture. Make one material with the
  texture into Base Color, Roughness about 0.85, and set the texture's filter
  to Nearest.
On any engine: nearest (no smoothing) filtering keeps the colours crisp.

LAYING FENCES AND WALLS
Four kinds: post-and-rail (fence_wood), wattle hurdles (fence_wattle), dry
stone (wall_stone) and the palisade. Each has a straight bay, a corner, an
end piece and a gate, and every piece goes at its own origin, nothing moved.
- A straight bay is 1 m long. It runs along +x from its origin on the line
  y=0, so a run is bays at (0,0,0), (1,0,0), (2,0,0)...
- A corner at (N,0,0) turns the run to the left: it fills x N..N+1 and
  y 0..1, and the next bay goes at (N+1,1,0) turned 90 degrees.
- A gate takes the place of one bay. The palisade's gate is two bays wide:
  lay it at x=N in place of the bays at N and N+1.
- An end piece finishes a run: at the far end's origin with no turn, and
  at the first bay's origin turned 180 degrees.
- Tops: wood 1.10 m, wattle 1.05, stone 0.95, palisade 2.30.

TOOLS, SIGNPOSTS AND THINGS THAT HANG
- Every hand tool has its origin in the middle of the fist: put the origin
  on the character's hand bone and it is held by its grip.
- The chain, the hook and the pulley have their origin where they hang
  from: put it on the beam or the hook above.
- The signposts' arms point along +x, -x, +y and -y, and their boards are
  blank for your own words. Turn a post in steps of 90 degrees to aim them
  down a road.

HOW THESE WERE MADE
Every model here was built by Python scripts in Blender, written with the help of an AI coding assistant. 6 of the 20 models started as 3D meshes generated by Tripo AI from AI-made concept art; the scripts then reshaped, cleaned, repainted and reduced them.
